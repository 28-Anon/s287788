### The Cuba call is already resolved — and it missed

As of August 2026 the US has **not** invaded Cuba. What did happen: surveillance
flights surged from February, heavy new sanctions, a near-total fuel blockade,
and enough alarm that Cubans were preparing for invasion. Lula said publicly
that Trump told him privately he has no intention of invading. August 2026
bilateral talks signalled Cuban willingness to negotiate.

**So: wrong on the event, directionally alive on the pressure.** That nuance is
the single most interesting thing in this whole file — see §5.

## 3. THE ACCURACY NUMBER IS DOING A LOT OF WORK

The headline is "76% accurate." Here is what sits under it:

- **351 predictions tracked** from 171 lectures
- **21 definitively resolved** — 16 right, 5 wrong → 76%
- **305 still pending**
- **16 leaning but unsettled**
- **9 "unverifiable by design"**

**Only about 6% of the predictions have actually resolved.** A 76% rate on 21
data points is a small-sample number being presented as a track record. It is
not evidence of a forecasting edge yet — it is 16 correct calls.

Worse, the trackers don't agree with themselves. Search surfaced "351 tracked /
76%", "382 tracked / 73%", "376 predictions", and "111 tracked" across pages of
the same ecosystem. **Inconsistent denominators are a red flag** — it means the
sample is being redrawn, and a rate you can restate is a rate you can tune.

### Credibility flags worth knowing before you build on this

- **The trackers are supporter-maintained.** No independent audit. The hit rate
  is self-reported by the fan ecosystem.
- **"Professor" is contested.** Coverage notes he styles himself Professor Jiang
  but is not a university professor.
- **Journalists flag conspiratorial elements** in some of the rhetoric.
- **"Unverifiable by design" is the tell.** Nine predictions that can never be
  scored is not an accident of phrasing; it's a category that protects a record.

None of this means he's wrong. Trump's return and the Iran escalation were real
calls, made publicly, in advance. It means **the marketing is stronger than the
evidence**, and if you repeat the marketing you inherit it.

## 4. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE CHANNEL

Three possible positions. They are not equally good.

**A. Repeat the predictions.** Lowest effort, worst position. You inherit every
miss, your face is on it, and you're the tenth channel doing it.

**B. Explain the method.** Better. Take the pressure-reading approach and apply
it yourself. Predictions become yours; so does the track record.

**C. Score the predictions honestly.** *This is the opening.* Every tracker in
this space is supporter-run and leads with hits. Nobody is making the video that
says "he said the US would invade Cuba in April. It's August. It didn't happen —
here's what did." That video is checkable, high-interest, and it makes you the
authority rather than a repeater.

C also solves the format problem: a scoring video is a **receipt**, not a
forecast, so it can't be wrong. You get the drama of prediction content with the
safety of reporting.

---

## 5. THE STRONGEST ANGLE

**"The pressure was right. The date was wrong."**

That's the honest reading of the Cuba call, and it's a genuinely interesting
idea rather than a dunk. Surveillance flights did surge. Sanctions did tighten.
The blockade is real. People on the island were preparing. The forecast caught
something true about the direction and got the event and the date wrong.

That's the most useful thing anyone can teach an audience about prediction:
**pressure is readable, timing usually isn't.** A channel built on that idea is
more durable than one built on calling dates, because it's true, it's teachable,
and it survives being checked.
