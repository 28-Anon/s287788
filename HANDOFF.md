# WHAT-IF SCRIPTS — FORMAT + IDEAS

Everything needed to write more of these from scratch. Self-contained: hand this
to any writer or model and they can produce a script without seeing the others.

---

## 1. THE FORMAT

Reverse-engineered from a TikTok that runs this structure (sword out a car
window at a tree, escalating to indestructible objects). 9:16, burned-in
captions, one caption per beat, ~3 seconds each.

### Structure — 22 beats, 69 seconds

| Beats | Job |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook.** See §2. |
| 2–5 | Premise, then the obvious answer, answered honestly and fast |
| ~6 | **PIVOT 1** — "BUT WHAT IF …?" |
| 7–9 | What that causes |
| ~10 | **PIVOT 2** — "BUT WHAT IF …?" |
| 11–14 | What that causes |
| 15 | **Payoff** — one line, landed on the payoff image |
| 16–21 | **Spoken close**, turned to camera |
| 22 | **The ask** — a question held on screen over the final image |
| — | Outro card, 3s |

Pivots can sit at 6/10, 6/11, 7/10 or 6/9 — the position flexes, the principle
doesn't.

### The engine: escalate by removing a constraint, never by advancing a clock

Each pivot takes away the objection the viewer is currently forming. That's the
whole mechanism. "But what if the car can't stop" → "but if the tree is
indestructible too".

**Never use time to escalate.** No "day two", "year six", "fifty years later".
If a premise seems to need a timeline, the pivots haven't been found yet. Ask:
*what two things would have to stop being true for this to have nowhere left to
go?* Remove those.

### Hard rules

1. **One location for the whole video.** The camera never leaves it. Anything
   else happens at the far end of that place, or through a window. This is also
   why one person can shoot it.
2. **3–9 words per caption.** Average about 6. Roughly 2 words per second.
3. **One idea per beat.** Two ideas means split it into two beats.
4. **Every beat either answers or escalates. Never explains.** The moment a beat
   teaches, retention drops.
5. **No jargon, no numbers.** Only things people feel: empty shelves, a queue,
   the lights going out. One number per script maximum.
6. **The payoff is carried by the image**, not the sentence over it.
7. **Never go silent.** The voice runs to 1:03. A wordless replay at the end is
   where people leave.

### The spoken close (beats 16–21)

Restate the payoff in plainer words → concede what complicates it → **make it
present tense** (this is the strongest move: the hypothetical becomes the
viewer's actual Tuesday) → then ask something you genuinely can't answer for
them. Beat 22 is that question, held on screen.

### The honesty rule — the one that gets broken most

**Every factual claim must survive being argued with in the comments.** Two
failure modes to watch, both of which happened repeatedly while writing these:

- **Picking a dramatic image, then reverse-engineering a justification.** If the
  picture came first and the reasoning second, it's probably wrong. Test each
  claim on its own before it earns its beat.
- **Over-claiming scope.** "Every payment is checked by AI" is false — plenty of
  contactless payments clear offline with no real-time check, much risk logic is
  rule-based, cash is checked by nothing. "Fraud checks run on AI" is true. The
  smaller true claim always lands harder than the big false one, because nobody
  can take it away from you.

Also: **after any round of edits, re-read the whole script end to end.**
Contradictions live in the gaps between edits — change a hook and the payoff can
silently stop matching it.

## 2. THE HOOK

Beat 1. Four types; write all four, shoot the best plus one alternate, post
both, keep whichever holds at 0:03. **Change only beats 1–2 between versions** —
same visuals, same audio, or the test tells you nothing. Judge on 3-second
retention, not likes.

| Type | What it does | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold premise** | States the hypothetical flat | Clear, safe, forgettable |
| **Drop-in** | Starts mid-event, no "imagine" | Loses people who scrolled in cold |
| **Outcome-first** | Gives away the ending; they stay for the *how* | Usually highest retention; wastes the twist if the middle is weak |
| **False comfort** | Opens like good news, then turns | The turn must arrive by 0:12 |

An outcome-first or drop-in hook gives the ending away, so **beat 2 must carry
the premise** the hook skipped.

## 3. THE ELEVEN IDEAS

Each: premise · one location · the two constraints removed · what it lands on.

**01 · What if nobody needed money** — *a single market stall*
Pivots: the stall never runs out (removes scarcity) → everyone arrives at once
(removes taking turns). Lands on: money is the answer to "who goes first", and
there is always a queue. Ask: *who should it be?*

**02 · What if every government disappeared** — *your street, from your front door*
Pivots: someone takes too much of the last clean water (removes goodwill) → the
next street wants it (removes isolation). Lands on: you rebuild a government in
four days without voting for it. Ask: *when do you stop paying?*

**03 · What if everyone became a millionaire** — *a car dealership forecourt*
Pivots: they build more (removes supply scarcity) → robots build them (removes
the labour needed). Lands on: everyone has a Ferrari and nobody wants one,
because half of wanting is wanting what others can't have. Ask: *would you still
want one?*

**04 · What if humanity stopped having children** — *a primary school classroom*
Pivots: machines do all the work (nothing decays) → nobody ever gets old
(nothing is lost). Lands on: a world that runs perfectly and permanently with
nothing new in it. Ask: *would you want to live there?*

**05 · What if there was no AI** — *a supermarket checkout*
Pivots: Google stops working too (search ranking is ML) → your money stops
moving (fraud checks are ML). Lands on: the whole shop stopped and nobody able
to look up why. Ask: *would you switch it off?* Play it completely neutral —
the second it picks a side, the comments argue about something you didn't say.

**06 · What if nobody could lie** — *a family dinner table*
Pivots: you could stay quiet (silence becomes the new lie) → you have to answer
(removes the last exit). Lands on: nobody wanted honesty, they wanted the right
to withhold — which is what privacy is. Ask: *who would you stop speaking to?*

**07 · What if nobody needed sleep** — *one bedroom, city through the window*
Pivots: everyone else gets the hours too (removes your advantage) → nobody ever
switches off (removes opting out). Lands on: sleep was the only stop nobody
could be competed out of. Ask: *would you take the eight hours?*

**08 · What if you could see how long you had left** — *a bus stop*
Pivots: you can change it (a fact becomes a decision) → everyone can see yours
(private becomes public). Lands on: it was never a number, it was a price — and
insurers already have a version of yours. Ask: *would it be better if everyone
could see it?*

**09 · What if nobody ever forgot anything** — *a parked car at night, two people*
Pivots: you never bring it up (you're still holding it) → they remember it too
(removes the asymmetry). Lands on: forgiveness relies on the sharpness fading;
remove the fading and you keep the forgiveness and lose the peace. Ask: *would
you give it back?*

**10 · What if pain didn't exist** — *a kitchen*
Pivots: you still heal (no scar, so no record) → you never look (removes your
last backup). Lands on: pain was the warning, not the enemy. Ask: *what is it
trying to say?* **This one is documented, not invented** — congenital
insensitivity to pain produces exactly these injuries. Most defensible of the
eleven.

**11 · What if you woke up without a phone** — *a railway platform*
Pivots: you don't need it anyway — cash, paper ticket, a watch (removes the
utility argument) → you remember one number (removes the last excuse; you ring
it and she still doesn't answer, because nobody answers unknown numbers). Lands
on: the phone holds your identity to people who already know you. Ask: *who
could you still reach?*

## 4. STARTING POINTS FOR NEW ONES

Premises that fit the engine — each needs one location and two removable
constraints found before writing:

- What if nobody could be anonymous
- What if everyone was exactly as clever as everyone else
- What if you could feel what you cause other people
- What if nothing could ever be copied
- What if everyone knew what everyone earned
- What if you could only ever tell the truth about yourself
- What if nobody could own land

For each, before writing a single beat: name the location, then name the two
things that would have to stop being true. If either is hard to name, the
premise isn't ready.
