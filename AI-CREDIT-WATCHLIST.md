# THE WATCHLIST — who breaks first if the money stops

Sources: Nvidia's Q2 FY2027 press release (primary), plus reporting on CoreWeave,
Nebius and Oracle. Where something is my reasoning rather than a reported fact,
it says so.

---

## 1. ARE THEY RAISING PRICES TO MANUFACTURE BEATS?

**No clear evidence, and the guidance argues against it.**

| Period | Gross margin | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Q2 FY26 (year ago) | 72.4% | |
| Q1 FY27 | 74.9% | |
| **Q2 FY27 (now)** | **75.0%** | H20 was a $180m *release* this time |
| **Q3 FY27 (guided)** | **74.0%** | **guided DOWN a full point** |

Margin is up 2.6 points year on year, but much of that is the *absence* of last
year's $4.5bn H20 charge, not pricing — that charge dragged the FY26 six-month
margin down to 66.6%.

And the decisive bit: **they guided margins down for next quarter.** A company
inflating prices to hit numbers does not do that. The likelier explanation for
74% is the Vera Rubin ramp — new products cost more to build early — plus
rising memory prices.

**But the instinct behind the question is right.** Something *is* flattering the
numbers. It just isn't price. It's that Nvidia is increasingly funding the
customers who buy from it, so some of the demand is its own money coming back.

---

## 2. WHO BREAKS FIRST

### Tier 1 — the neoclouds. Most fragile by a distance.

**CoreWeave** — named in Nvidia's release as running Vera Rubin racks.
- Total debt **$35bn** as of 30 June 2026, up from $22.7bn a quarter earlier
- Net interest expense **$640m in one quarter**, more than double a year ago
- Variable rate averaging around **11%**
- Q1 2026 free cash flow **negative $4.71bn**
- Repayments began January 2026
- Fell 12% on a day the 30-year Treasury hit 5.32%

This is the single most exposed name. It borrows at a floating rate to buy
depreciating GPUs and rents them out. Every rate rise hits it twice.

**Nebius** — also named in Nvidia's release. Stock plunged alongside CoreWeave in
July on credit-swap costs. Notably, it is trying a different model: asking
customers to prepay before infrastructure is built. That is a sensible response
to exactly this risk, and worth watching as a tell for how hard financing has got.

### Tier 2 — the big borrower

**Oracle** — raising debt aggressively to fund data centre expansion, with a
huge OpenAI backlog. A mega-cap, so it will not fail, but it is the most
leveraged of the large players and would be repriced hardest.

### Tier 3 — the ones Nvidia is directly tied to

**OpenAI** — Nvidia's $100bn commitment. If it cannot raise its next round on
acceptable terms, that is the event.
**Groq** — Nvidia paid $2.94bn related to Groq in this quarter's financing
activities, and Groq 3 LPX is now in Nvidia's product line.
**SB Energy / SoftBank** — Ohio campus partnership named in the release.

### Tier 4 — the suppliers, hit second

**TSMC** (makes the chips), **SK hynix** (memory partnership named in the
release), **Foxconn** (named), plus the power and cooling chain — Vertiv, Eaton,
GE Vernova. These lag the neoclouds by a quarter or two.

### Tier 5 — the lease holders

Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, Alphabet and Oracle hold roughly **$1.09 trillion of
data centre leases that have not started**. They can simply not start them. That
is the shock absorber — and also how the slowdown transmits to everyone above.

## 3. WHEN

The industry term is the **neocloud refinancing wall: 2026 to 2028.** Debt taken
on cheaply is maturing into a market where the 30-year is above 5%.

| When | What to look at |
|---|---|
| **Late Oct 2026** | Blackstone, Apollo, KKR Q3 results — any comment on AI lending appetite |
| **Late Nov 2026** | Nvidia Q3. Does the cash gap widen again? Margin below 74%? |
| **Nov 2026 – Feb 2027** | The $500bn platforms either reach definitive agreements or go quiet |
| **Each quarter** | CoreWeave interest expense and free cash flow |
| **Continuous** | The 30-year Treasury. Above 5% is the pressure gauge |

My reasoning, not a reported fact: deals of that size typically take three to
six months to paper, so **silence past February 2027 is the signal.**

---

## 4. WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENS, IN ORDER

1. A neocloud cannot refinance at a workable rate. Orders get deferred, not cancelled — deferral is quieter and hurts later.
2. Nvidia's receivables get harder to collect. It is owed $63bn.
3. Its equity stakes fall, so the $7.8bn of paper profit reverses.
4. Hyperscalers quietly do not start those leases. No announcement.
5. Suppliers — TSMC, SK hynix, power equipment — see order cuts a quarter later.
6. Private credit funds mark down AI loans. This is where it stops being a tech story and becomes a credit story.
7. Index funds fall, because these companies are the largest weights in the S&P. That is how it reaches ordinary pensions.

**The whole chain runs on one question: can the AI companies pay for the chips
without borrowing more?** Today, largely, they cannot.

---

## 5. REVISING MY EARLIER ESTIMATE

I previously put "it breaks" at roughly 15%. **On this evidence I would move it
to around 25%.** CoreWeave carrying $35bn at about 11% floating with negative
free cash flow and repayments already started is materially more fragile than I
assumed when I gave that number.

Still the least likely of the three outcomes. But no longer a tail risk.
