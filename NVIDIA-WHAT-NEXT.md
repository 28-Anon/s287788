# WHAT THIS COULD MEAN — speculation, clearly labelled

The numbers in the script are all from the filing. **This page is not.** These
are my own estimates and reasoning, and the percentages are judgement, not data.
Say so on camera if you use them.

---

## The one thing to understand first

A company can be selling brilliantly and still be running short of cash. That
sounds impossible, so here's how it happens:

You sell a chip for a million pounds. You book a million pounds of sales
immediately. But the customer pays in ninety days. Meanwhile you already paid to
build the chip. So on paper you're rich, and in the bank you're not.

Now add the second bit. Some of your customers are new AI companies that don't
have a million pounds. So **you invest in them**, and they use your money to buy
your chips. Your sales go up. Your cash goes down. And you now own a piece of a
company whose main asset is your own product.

That is what the filing shows. It is not fraud and it is not unusual in a boom.
It just means the growth is being funded rather than earned.

---

## Three ways this goes

### 1. It works — roughly 45%

AI demand keeps compounding. The startups Nvidia backed turn into real
businesses with real revenue. They pay their bills. The stakes Nvidia holds
become worth far more than it paid. In hindsight, financing your own market
looks like genius rather than desperation.

This is exactly what Huang is arguing: *"compute is revenue"*, AI is *"doing
useful work"*. If tokens really are profitable now, the circle closes honestly
and everybody who called it a bubble looks silly.

**Watch for:** the AI companies announcing real paying customers, not funding rounds.

### 2. It stalls slowly — roughly 40%

The most likely single outcome, and the most boring. Growth carries on but
decelerates. Nvidia quietly stops buying stakes. Margins keep drifting down —
they've already guided from 75% to 74%. The share price stops going up without
ever crashing, and in two years people say the AI boom "cooled off" rather than
"burst".

**Watch for:** margins guided down again next quarter, and the equity purchases
line shrinking.

### 3. It breaks — roughly 15%

One large AI customer can't raise its next round and can't pay. Nvidia writes
down what it's owed. The stakes fall, so the paper profits reverse — that eight
billion goes the other way. Because Nvidia now has real debt, it can't simply
absorb it. Lenders reprice everything AI-related at once, and the trillion
dollars of data centre leases that were signed but not started quietly don't
happen.

**Watch for:** any large AI company failing to close a funding round.

## The actual tell — and it isn't Nvidia

On 10 August, Nvidia announced financing platforms with **Apollo, BlackRock,
Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR** to mobilise **over $500 billion**
of other people's money for the AI buildout. The press release says *"subject to
definitive agreements"* — meaning it isn't signed yet.

**If those deals sign, this runs for years.** Half a trillion of private credit
means the buildout doesn't depend on Nvidia's own balance sheet any more.

**If they quietly don't, that is your signal.** It would mean the most
sophisticated lenders in the world looked at these returns and declined. That
tells you more than any earnings report will.

That is the single most useful thing to watch over the next few months, and
almost nobody is watching it.
