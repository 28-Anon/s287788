# The Format

Reverse-engineered from the reference TikTok (sword/tree/car, @tl_hd7, 76s).
This is the engine. Every script below runs on it.

## The engine

1. **HOOK (0-3s)** — one concrete image, present tense. Never an abstract
   question. "Imagine money stopped working tomorrow", not "What is money?"
2. **THE OBVIOUS ANSWER (3-12s)** — answer the easy version honestly and fast.
   This buys trust. The reference got the sword ripping out of your hands right.
3. **THE ESCALATION (12-48s)** — "But what if X couldn't happen?"
   Remove one constraint. Answer. Remove the next. 3-5 rungs.
   Each rung must feel like the viewer's own next question.
4. **THE TERMINAL STATE (48-54s)** — the system has nowhere left to go.
5. **THE PAYOFF (last 6s)** — one line that reframes the whole thing.

## Beat rules

- **3-8 words per caption.** ALL CAPS, centred, held ~3s. ~20 beats for a 60s video.
- **One new idea per beat.** If a beat contains two, split it.
- **Every beat either answers or escalates. Never explains.**
  The moment a beat teaches, retention drops.
- **No numbers, no jargon, no mechanisms.** Only comparisons people already
  feel in their body. Empty shelves. A queue. Lights going out.
- **The turn:** the last third reveals that the thing you deleted comes back,
  or was never the thing you thought it was. That's the whole payload.

## The one thing to do better than the reference

The reference lies at the finish — "energy has nowhere to escape → nuclear
explosion" is wrong by about seven orders of magnitude, and you can feel the
video swap thinking for spectacle.

For these four premises the *true* answer is the more shocking one. Don't
invent a bigger ending than reality; find the real one and land it hard.
That's the difference between a video that gets watched and one that gets sent.

## Production

- 9:16. Captions burned in, centred, high on the frame, thick white with shadow.
- **Proofread every caption.** The reference has six typos in 45 seconds
  ("WHOUD", "A HULT", "YOU HANDS"). It reads as slop and caps who shares it.
- Visuals carry no information — they carry mood. The caption does the work.
- End on a hard cut to black before the payoff line's last word lands.
