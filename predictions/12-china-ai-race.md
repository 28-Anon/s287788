# 12 — CHINA VS AMERICA, THE AI RACE

**Scene:** A table, two phones side by side. Same question typed into both.  
**Runtime:** 1:03 (131 words)

---

America is spending eight times more than China on artificial intelligence, to stay about eight months ahead.

The five biggest American tech companies will spend around six hundred and ninety billion dollars this year. China's four biggest will spend about a hundred billion.

Eight times less. However, an American government test put China's best model roughly eight months behind America's best. On some tasks, level.

And here is the part that matters. Running the same job on a Chinese model costs about fourteen dollars a month. On a top American one, over a thousand.

So if cheap and slightly behind is good enough, that money never earns itself back.

Which is exactly why Nvidia is lending its own customers the money to keep buying.

So who is actually winning this race?

---

## Hook variants

- **A · Distinction** · **IN SCRIPT** · **LEAD WITH THIS**  
  America is ahead in the AI race. That is not the same as winning it.
- **B · Contradiction**  
  You cannot call it winning if it costs you eight times more.
- **C · Cold statement**  
  America is eight months ahead in AI, and paying eight times more for those eight months.
- **D · Unreported fact**  
  China is spending eight times less on AI, and almost nobody is asking why that isn't showing.

**Why that one.** "Ahead" and "winning" are not the same thing, and the whole
script rests on that gap. Being marginally in front at eight times the cost,
while the gap closes, is not winning — it is paying to lose slowly. Drawing that
line in the first twelve words is what makes the closing question land instead
of sounding rhetorical.
