# THE STACK — the project's reference frame

Supplied by the channel owner, not from my research. Everything I write should be
positioned against this rather than treating "AI" as one thing.

---

## The five layers

| | Layer | Where we are |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Energy** | built out first |
| 2 | **Chips** | **← we are here** |
| 3 | **Infrastructure** | next |
| 4 | **Models** | later — with growing emphasis on world models |
| 5 | **Apps** | last |

The money moves up the stack in order. That means most of what gets called
"the AI trade" is actually a bet on one layer, and the layers do not rise or
fall together.

## Layer 2 splits in two

**Memory** and **logic**. Different companies, different economics, different
cycles. Most coverage collapses them.

**Nvidia is a logic company.** One of three main US-listed logic players
alongside **Broadcom** and **AMD** — and it does not manufacture. It designs and
outsources fabrication.

**SMH** (the semiconductor ETF) holds 25 of the largest US-listed companies
across semiconductor production, design and equipment. That is the actual
shape of layer 2, not one ticker.

---

## WHAT THIS CHANGES IN WORK ALREADY DONE

### 1. I have been treating Nvidia as the AI trade. It isn't.
It is one company, in one half, of one layer, that doesn't even make the
product. Every script framing Nvidia as a proxy for AI needs that caveat.

### 2. The margin guide-down probably isn't what I guessed.
I attributed Nvidia guiding gross margin from 75% to 74% to the Vera Rubin
ramp. **Memory cost is the better explanation** — memory prices were a flagged
theme going into the print, and Nvidia announced a multiyear memory partnership
with SK hynix in the same release. A logic designer's margin gets squeezed when
memory prices rise, and that is a layer-2 story I could not see while treating
chips as one category.

### 3. Energy is upstream, not downstream.
I listed power and cooling firms as "tier 4 suppliers" that get hit *after* the
neoclouds. If energy is layer 1, it is **underneath everything** — it gets built
first and it constrains what can be built above it. That reverses the order.

It also makes one thing far more important than I treated it: reporting that
**China's advantage is cheap energy**. If layer 1 is the real constraint, a
country with abundant cheap power has a structural edge at the base of the stack
that no export control touches. That may matter more than the 8-to-1 spending
gap.

### 4. "The AI bubble" is a layer question, not a market question.
A correction in layer 2 does not have to touch layers 4 and 5, and vice versa.
Every crash claim should specify **which layer**.

## OPEN QUESTIONS FOR THE OWNER

1. **Where do the hyperscalers sit?** Microsoft, Google, Amazon and Meta buy
   chips (2), build infrastructure (3) and train models (4). Do they span, or
   do you place them at 3?
2. **Is manufacturing its own sub-layer?** TSMC makes what Nvidia designs.
   Design and fabrication behave very differently. Same layer or split?
3. **Where does networking go?** A large share of Broadcom's business.
4. **Why do world models matter more later?** Flagged as a future emphasis at
   layer 4 — worth understanding before scripting it.
5. **What breaks the chain?** If money climbs the stack in order, a stall at
   layer 2 should stop layers 3 to 5 being built. Is that the real transmission
   mechanism rather than the credit story I have been building?
