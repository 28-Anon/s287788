# WHAT THE SUPPLY CHAIN CHANGES ABOUT THE EARNINGS

Six things I did not see until your industry notes sat next to the Q2 numbers.
Marked **[solid]** where it follows directly from disclosed facts, and
**[hypothesis]** where it is my inference with a stated test.

---

## 1 · Nvidia's revenue is now forecastable from TSMC's capex, not from AI demand · [solid]

CoWoS packaging is sold out through 2026 and Nvidia has booked roughly 60% of
global capacity. Lead times are 52–78 weeks.

That means next year's revenue is not a demand question. It is an arithmetic
question about how many packages TSMC can bond and how many of those are
Nvidia's. TSMC publishes capex and capacity targets. **You can build a ceiling
for Nvidia's FY2028 revenue off a supplier's disclosure**, which is not how
anybody covers this stock.

It also means the $108bn guide is a **supply statement dressed as a demand
statement.** They are telling you what can be built, not what could be sold.

---

## 2 · The cash flow collapse may be prepayment, not distress · [hypothesis]

I flagged free cash flow down 56% and receivables up $24.6bn as a warning.

Add the lead times and there is an innocent explanation. To hold 60% of 2026–27
packaging you commit capital 12–18 months ahead of the revenue it produces.
Cash out now, revenue later, by design.

**The test:** Nvidia discloses purchase commitments and supply obligations in
the 10-Q. If that line has ballooned in step with the cash outflow, this is
prepayment and I over-called it. If it has not, the cash went somewhere else
and the original concern stands. That single number decides it.

---

## 3 · The circular financing has a physical reason, not just a promotional one · [hypothesis → strong]

Earlier framing: Nvidia funds its customers to manufacture demand.

Better framing: Nvidia has **already paid for** packaging slots it cannot
cancel and cannot store. A booked slot you do not fill is money burnt. Funding
OpenAI is not conjuring demand from nothing — it is **guaranteeing offtake for
capacity already bought.**

That is a more rational act than it looked. It is also a more concentrated
risk: if the funded customer cannot take delivery, Nvidia eats the booking. The
$100bn is not marketing spend. It is insurance on a supply commitment.

---

## 4 · Booking 60% of world packaging is capacity denial as much as capacity purchase · [hypothesis]

Broadcom's custom XPUs for Google, Meta and ByteDance go through **the same
TSMC CoWoS line.** They are not an alternative supply route. They compete for
the identical bottleneck.

So the hyperscaler shift to custom silicon does not reduce pressure on the
constraint. It changes who wins the slot. And Nvidia holding 60% of it means
every XPU Broadcom wants to ship is competing for the remainder.

Whether or not it is the intent, **the effect is that Nvidia's booking limits
how fast its replacements can arrive.** The competitive moat next year is not
architecture. It is a purchase order placed in 2025.

---

## 5 · Power scarcity is not an alternative to the credit story. It is the trigger for it · [solid]

I had two separate paths to a bust: financing tightens, or power fails to
arrive. They are one path.

Grid queues sort by who already holds a position. Hyperscalers have one.
Leveraged neoclouds lease space and sit further back. So the power constraint
lands **hardest on exactly the balance sheets in the credit watchlist** —
CoreWeave and the like, carrying floating-rate debt against revenue they cannot
earn until a substation exists.

And layer-1 equipment prices ran 20%+ higher in H1 2026. That cost falls on the
operator, not on Nvidia. **Nvidia's margins are insulated while its customers'
unit economics quietly degrade.** Which is precisely why the seller looks
healthy right up to the point the buyers cannot make the numbers work.

---

## 6 · The real bubble question, stated properly · [solid framing]

Your model ladder makes this precise in a way "is AI a bubble" never does.

Everything paying for this today is **stage two** — text. Chatbots and text
inference. The capex being raised is justified by **stages four and five** —
agents and world models — which are early. The World-Gymnast result says stage
five works; it does not say it ships revenue.

So the question is not whether AI is real. It is a **timing** question:

> Does stage four and five revenue arrive before the stage-two-funded debt
> matures?

Debt raised now is three to five year paper. Robotics and world-model revenue
at scale is plausibly longer than that. **That gap is the entire risk**, and it
is a much harder statement than anything in the bubble discourse.
